ChatGPT Ads Manager is the beta console behind advertising inside ChatGPT. What it lets you build, which number arrives late, and the one thing it cannot tell you.

What the ChatGPT Ads Manager beta actually does

The help documentation is refreshingly plain about scope. OpenAI Ads Manager Beta is described as the place to create, launch and manage campaigns for ads inside ChatGPT, with performance monitoring and updates handled in one interface, and the company states outright that it is a beta product that will keep evolving. Three workflows are supported end to end: creating and managing campaigns, monitoring performance, and managing account settings. The onboarding path is equally direct. You sign up on the advertising site, get taken into the console, create your account, then build a campaign with a budget and a goal, add ad details, and launch. Step-by-step help articles cover account setup and the first campaign. Country availability sits in its own help article and is described as still expanding, so confirm your market there rather than assuming access.

Two ways in, and one gap in the CSV

Campaigns can be built one at a time through a guided workflow, or at scale through bulk upload for the campaign types that support it. Ad details follow the same pattern: bulk upload them, or create each one directly in the tool. There is a specific limitation worth knowing before you plan a migration, because it will cost you an afternoon otherwise. CSV upload does not currently support creating product-feed campaigns, so anything catalogue-driven has to be built through the interface. If your existing search or shopping setup is feed-based and you were expecting to export, reshape and import it in one pass, budget for manual work instead. The word currently is doing real work in that sentence, since the feature set is explicitly described as growing over time, but plan against what ships today.

The number that arrives late

Reporting covers impressions, clicks and spend across campaigns, ad groups and individual ads, available as table views, charts and CSV exports. The timing is uneven in a way that trips people up. Impressions, clicks and click-through rate can appear within minutes, which feels reassuringly fast. Spend updates later, and the documentation warns directly that a temporary zero spend value does not necessarily mean no charges have accrued. Read that twice before you let anyone judge a launch on its first hour. A campaign showing heavy clicks and no cost is not a bargain, it is an incomplete row. Anyone building an automated pacing rule, an alert or a dashboard on top of these exports should treat the spend column as eventually consistent and hold off on decisions until it settles.

Accounts, permissions and the API underneath

Account-level settings cover members and permissions, billing details, change logs and API keys, which is the detail most write-ups skip. Keys point at a documented advertising API, meaning the console is one surface over something programmable rather than the only way in. For a small team that matters less than the change log, which is the thing you will want at the end of a bad week when somebody wants to know who edited a budget. Permissions being present at all is a good sign for agency use. The public trust commitments are also worth noting: ads carry clear labels in the experience and stay separate from the assistant's answer. Named early advertisers include Newegg, Best Buy, Lowe's and VistaPrint, with a Newegg marketing manager quoted about being part of the research conversation.

The blind spot every console shares

An ads manager is a mirror. It tells you what you spent, what you got and what to change, and it is silent about the market you are competing in. It will not show you which rivals are bidding for the same intent, what their creative says this month, or which creators are already shaping opinion in your category before anyone reaches a chat window. That information exists, it is largely public, and reading it consistently is how teams decide what to test rather than merely how much to spend. Running a first campaign sensibly

  1. Check your country first
    Availability lives in its own help article and is described as expanding with testing. Confirm your market is covered before writing briefs or booking creative production time.
  2. Build one campaign by hand
    Use the guided workflow for the first campaign even if you plan to bulk upload later. You learn the field names, and any feed-based work has to be built manually anyway.
  3. Ignore spend for the first day
    Clicks and click-through rate arrive within minutes while spend catches up later. Judge nothing until the cost column has settled, whatever the early dashboard appears to say.

    Add members with the right permissions and keep an eye on the change log from the start. Retrofitting access control after a mis-set budget is a much worse afternoon.

FAQ

Is ChatGPT Ads Manager actually available?

Yes. The advertising site takes you into OpenAI Ads Manager Beta after signup, where you create an account, build campaigns and monitor results. The vendor describes it as a beta whose capabilities are limited today and will expand, and country availability is documented separately and described as still growing with testing.

Can I bulk upload my existing campaigns?

Partly. Bulk CSV upload works for supported campaign types and for ad details, but the documentation states that CSV upload does not currently support creating product-feed campaigns. Anything catalogue-driven needs building through the interface, so plan manual work into any migration from a feed-based setup elsewhere.

Why does my spend show zero when I have clicks?

Because the metrics update at different speeds. Impressions, clicks and click-through rate can appear within minutes, while spend updates later, and the help documentation warns that a temporary zero does not mean no charges have accrued. Wait for the cost column to settle before drawing any conclusion about efficiency.

Is there an API, or only the console?

Account settings include API keys alongside members, permissions, billing details and change logs, and those keys point at a documented advertising API. So the console is one surface over something programmable, which matters if you plan to pipe reporting into your own dashboards rather than living in exports.

How do the ads appear to users?

The advertising site states that ads are clearly identified in the experience and remain distinct from the assistant's response, with the stated aim of keeping advertiser usefulness and user trust separate. Named early advertisers include Newegg, Best Buy, Lowe's and VistaPrint, with a Newegg marketing manager quoted about showing up during product research.

How do I see what competitors are doing here?

You cannot, from inside the console. An ads manager reports your own activity and nothing about the market around it.
